Grounding Context

Codebase Context

  • Mounting point. ReportType (3 shipping cases) drives the top selector row, the zoom row's exhaustive switch and ChartView.body; adding a case lights a tab. Decided: lab tabs render on a new native LabChartView (scrollable axes, visible-domain zoom, hour snapping, chartXSelection scrub); the shipping chart body gets one added switch arm and nothing else; the marker lane is hidden on lab tabs; everything sits behind an off-by-default showChartLab setting.
  • Data windows today. One day max (selected day or last 24 h) for the chart; notes and heart rate load the exact calendar day, meals/insulin/exercise a day or 24 h, IOB an independent DIA lookback — four sources, three window rules. Multi-day reads exist only on the clinic-report path: ClinicReportBuilder.hourlyPatterns (median/p25/p75 × 24 h, unit-tested, no on-screen consumer) and getClinicReportData(days:) (one asyncRead, 14/30/90 d).
  • Unused seams. mealSeries + MealDatapoint.carbs computed every render, rendered nowhere; computeMealOverlayDelta / mealImpactDeltaColor (tested, used only in the tap sheet); detectMealConfounders; Ratio Lab's meal-exclusion taxonomy; hypoEpisodes computes episode boundaries and throws them away; the shipping chart's onEnded clears every selection unconditionally on release and its tap branch is a no-op.
  • Life factors. Journal notes carry SICK / STRESSED / SLUGGISH / OTHER tags plus free text (timestamps only, no end, no edit path); exercise entries have start/end; hourly heart rate is read from HealthKit and drawn as a magenta line projected onto the glucose axis; sleep and steps are not read yet (two added read types on an existing HealthKit authorisation).
  • AI today. Claude Haiku for food analysis and the Daily Digest; structured output already enforced via output_config JSON schema; sanitizeUserText escapes prompt-injection characters; DigestInsight.parse is a lenient first-brace/last-brace scan the chart grammar must not inherit; consent copy currently says readings are sent.
  • Design system & safety. DOS amber tokens only; no glow inside Chart{}; .dosCard/.dosHeader chrome; ten StyleGuard source-scan rules. Ratio Lab's discipline governs every derived number: no dosing language, every figure ships with its N, two fixed disclaimers.
  • Past learnings that bite here. Chart height from GeometryReader, never UIScreen; branch heights pinned or layouts jump; native chrome fights DOS theming; user text escaped before any prompt; reuse IOBCalculator, never re-derive decay; reactive precompute belongs in middleware; every secondary fetch must cover the primary window; loading publishers must be total.
  • External context. AGP (Clarity 5.0, LibreView) is glucose-only and clock-locked; Loop/Trio show IOB/COB as numbers, never as predicted-vs-actual curves; Nightscout's amount-sized dots are well-loved; Clarity/LibreView users complain about no tap-to-inspect and no arbitrary-window scrub; Apple Health pairs a period picker with a scrub lollipop and narrated Highlights; NL2VIS systems (LIDA, Data Formulator, Vega-Lite, Draco) keep the LLM emitting a validated spec; Whoop/Oura users prize contributing-factor breakdowns; F1 telemetry, Strava form curves, DAW timelines and flight recorders are the working analogies for "one cursor across many tracks" and "the gap between curves is the signal".

Topic Axes

  • 1 · The event layer on the day chart — how meals, doses, exercise appear (size, shape, colour, labels) and how each connects to the glucose response that followed.
  • 2 · Patterns across days — recurring behaviours over weeks: percentile bands, day overlays, hourly heatmaps, splits by condition.
  • 3 · Life factors as data — stress, sickness, sluggishness, exercise, heart rate, sleep, steps, notes: how they enter and how they are drawn beside glucose.
  • 4 · Interaction and feel — Health-style scrub, periods, snapping, tap-to-inspect, haptics, motion, DOS amber craft; engaging yet professional.
  • 5 · AI in the loop — "explain this chart", "tell the AI what to build", consent and safety framing, narrated highlights.

1.

Instrument cursors — sticky scrub, A/B range, fixed readout, detached follow

axis 4 · interaction and feel
Description
The lab chart's gesture vocabulary, fixed once for every tab. One finger scrubs (chartXSelection) and the cursor persists after release. A long-press pins cursor A; a second scrub sets B; tapping a meal, dose or exercise mark pre-sets the range to its response window. A fixed-height monospace readout strip sits below the chart — never a floating card under the finger — and prints every track at the cursor or between the cursors: A 12:04 → B 13:44 · Δt 1h40 · G 142→96 (−46) · MIN 94 MAX 148 · IN 3.0U · CARBS 0g · HR 71→96 · n=20. An explicit FOLLOW ● state at the right edge: scrolling back detaches it (◂ 3 NEW) and new readings never move the visible domain until tapped. A light selection tick as the cursor crosses an event mark, a medium impact crossing the active alarm bounds, both night-mute gated. The same readout contract, applied to several stacked tracks with honest y-axes (glucose, IOB/COB, HR, steps, sleep stages), is how the factors wave draws streams that cannot be projected onto the glucose axis.

Rationale
This is the literal "Apple-Health-like" ask, and it is where Swift Charts' limits bite hardest: on a scrollable chart the built-in selection gesture is long-press-then-drag, the value/range bindings reset on release, and a two-step A/B is not the built-in range gesture. Prototyping the arbitration in week one means every later tab inherits a working contract instead of a broken one.
Downsides
Gesture arbitration (scroll vs long-press scrub vs A/B vs pinch) is real engineering, not styling; sticky state needs mirroring into local state; pinch zoom must co-set the scroll position or the view re-anchors. Vertical budget at phone height caps stacked tracks at about three by default.

2.

One whole-system window — continuous timeline, per-stream coverage, the night made whole

axis 3 · life factors as data
Description
The lab chart scrolls one continuous 90-day timeline with a pinchable 24 h visible domain and midnight snap, so the day pager and selectedDate have no job on lab tabs. One action, .loadLabWindow(interval, streams), handled by one middleware that fetches every stream for the same interval (GRDB glucose, meals, insulin, exercise, notes; HealthKit heart rate, sleep, steps), catches per-stream failures, and emits a snapshot carrying coverage per stream and a shared 60-second sample grid (glucose with an observed mask, IOB, COB, HR) so any cursor readout is an index lookup and derived lines are array arithmetic. Its visible face is a POST-style strip under every lab chart — GLU 96% · INS 4 · MEAL 3 · EX — · HR ✓ · SLEEP n/a · NOTES 1 — where each cell is loaded, empty-in-window, or unavailable-tap-to-Settings. The first surface it makes possible is a NIGHT view, 20:00→10:00 straddling midnight: glucose, sleep stages as block sparklines, the evening dose's IOB tail and the last meal's ribbon carried across midnight, heart rate, and a header IN BED 23:10 · ASLEEP 6H40 · AWAKE ×2. A wake-anchored modal day becomes a toggle on the pattern tab later.

Rationale
Every later idea sits on this: the sweep needs 90 days of meal-aligned glucose, regimes need cross-midnight bands, the factors wave needs one window contract for GRDB plus HealthKit, and the cursor readout needs O(1) lookups. Two documented silent-failure classes become an on-screen state in an idiom the aesthetic already owns.
Downsides
Swift Charts does not virtualise: a 90-day domain with tens of thousands of marks will not scroll unless data is windowed to the visible domain plus margin and drawn with iOS 18 vectorized plots. Largest single piece of platform work in the set; the grid must never fabricate values across sensor gaps. Lifting the IOB sampler out of the shipping view is a twin-code-path refactor that needs an explicit decision.

3.

The response kernel — one cause→effect primitive, with the graded live meal ribbon as its first instance

axis 1 · the event layer
Description
Lift the two-hour meal delta into an anchor-agnostic ResponseWindow(anchor:, lead: 15 min, lag: 2 h, summary: .extremum(.max)) returning ResponseSummary { baseline, extremum, delta, timeToExtremum, n, isLowConfidence, confounders }. Meals use the max over 2 h; exercise the min over 3 h; a sleep interval the dawn rise; a stress regime a slope over its band — one ribbon renderer and one caption serve them all, and every ribbon ships with its N by construction. Its first instance on LAB: MEALS: the moment a carb-sized mark lands on the curve, a ribbon grows rightward under the readings, stepping through the existing green/amber/red tiers as the running delta crosses 30 and 60 mg/dL, with a +47 · 38 MIN · 7 RDG readout and the unelapsed remainder as a dotted stub; at window close it hardens. Meals Ratio Lab would exclude are hatched and dimmed with the exclusion tag as the label (NO BOLUS, STACKED, CORR, HYPO), and the bolus Ratio Lab already pairs to the meal is joined by a thin bracket 45g ⟷ 4U — a pairing, never a ratio.

4.

Sweep & Laps — the event-locked response overlay

axis 2 · patterns across days
Description
A lab mode where the x-axis is minutes since the anchor (−30 … +240) and y is the change from the −15 min baseline — the oscilloscope's trigger and the F1 lap overlay. Two entrances. Sweep: every meal in the 7/30/90-day window is one sweep aligned at t=0, brightness stepping down with age through the amber tiers (opacity, never glow), the median sweep bright, p25–p75 per five-minute relative bin, chips by carb bucket (≤15 / 16–45 / 46–80 / >80 g), time-of-day and CLEAN ONLY, caption N=23 SWEEPS · 14 CLEAN. Laps: tap a meal mark on the day chart and its three to five most similar past meals (same carb bucket ±25 %, same time of day ±90 min, clean only, found from the whole meal table) ghost behind today's trace, readout TODAY +62 · TWINS MEDIAN +41 (n=4), with a WHY THESE row. The same [ResponseSummary] read renders two more ways as sub-modes: a shot-chart grid (carb bucket × 4-hour block, cells tinted by median delta with N, block glyphs) and an outcome ledger (one monospace row per event, sortable by effect). Bridge from idea 1: after measuring any interval by hand, SWEEP THIS overlays every event of the same kind with that lead/lag. Demo mode: a clip-mask replay plays all laps from t=0 together.

direct:MealImpact.swift:10-40 — persisted per meal: baselineGlucose: Int?, peakGlucose: Int, deltaMgDL: Int, timeToPeakMinutes: Int, isClean: Bool; MealOverlayLogic.swift — var isClean: Bool { !hasCorrectionBolus && !hasExercise && !hasStackedMeal }; ClinicReport.swift — static func percentile(_ sortedValues: [Int], _ p: Double) -> Int "Linear-interpolation percentile (type-7 / numpy default)"; RatioEstimator.swift:82-110 — MealExclusionReason; PersonalFood.swift:17,46 — observationCount, avgDeltaMgDL; CLAUDE.md — the meal search reaches the whole MealEntry table. external: Tektronix digital-phosphor persistence; stimulus-locked ERP averaging (Luck); F1 telemetry overlays; Goldsberry's hexbin shot charts; research CGM papers plot post-prandial overlays, no consumer app does. reasoned: AGP answers "what does 15:00 look like", never "what does a 60 g meal do to me"; meals do not recur at the same minute, so a clock-locked modal day washes out event structure — locking to the event is the only way to average it.
Rationale
The highest-novelty, highest-value answer in the set to "no cause and effect" and "can't see meal size": meal size becomes a dimension, the stable shape of the user's own absorption emerges, and the strays are the confounded meals. It redefines the patterns wave as two modal views — clock-locked and event-locked — and gives the COB model a place to be drawn against observed sweeps before it ever touches the day chart.
Downsides
A relative-minutes axis is numeric, so this is its own chart, not an overlay on the Date-axis day chart. Persisted meal impacts stop at 30 days, so 90-day sweeps recompute through the kernel over a new "glucose ±4 h around N anchors" read. Twin search is a similarity query with real edge cases. Sparse cells in the grid need honest gating.

The pattern comes to the day — your own band under today, out-of-band flags, long-press drill

axis 2 · patterns across days
Description
Instead of a tab the user switches to, the lab day chart draws the last 30 days' p25–p75 (then p5–p95) hourly band as a faint ghost behind today's curve, so every excursion is read against "your usual for this hour". Hours where today leaves the band get a DOS tick on the axis and a one-line card 15:00 · +38 VS USUAL · n=27 DAYS; the band's widest or highest hours carry a PATTERN HOUR tag with their sample count. Long-press an excursion → SAME HOUR · 14 D: the ±2 h window around the cursor replayed as fourteen dim day-traces with today's in amber and a count line 9 OF 14 DAYS > 180 HERE · n=612. Optional RANK mode on the pattern tab plots today as a percentile of the user's own hour-of-day distribution. No tab switch, no AI, computed offline from the tested percentile function. When a regime band is open (idea 6), the ghost band can switch to the matching cohort — "your usual on STRESSED days" — with its own N.

6.

The residual asks for the note — then notes become regimes

axis 3 · life factors as data
Description
Two moves in sequence, density first, then duration. Residual: the lab flags each excursion as explained (a meal, dose, exercise, regime or note sits in its window) or not; an unexplained rise or fall — say +45 mg/dL across 14:00–15:30 with nothing logged — is drawn as a hatched amber segment with a ? glyph, and tapping it opens the existing journal-note sheet with the time picker already set to the excursion start and the tag row focused. The detector only marks the absence of a logged cause; it never proposes one. Regimes: a SICK / STRESSED / SLUGGISH note opens a status effect — a translucent hatched band from its timestamp forward (per-tag defaults: SICK until the next note or 24 h, STRESSED 4 h, SLUGGISH rest of day; band edge draggable), closed by the next note, a one-tap BACK TO NORMAL, or the next wake, when the lab shows a one-tap STILL SICK? Y/N row. A HealthKit night under 6 h becomes a SHORT SLEEP band over the following day. Condition splits then use band membership (a day is SICK if ≥ 6 h under regime), so every split has a defensible N. Regimes are derived from notes, never stored as a new model.

This is how "stress level and other factors in life" become tangible rather than decorative: the residual raises tag density where it matters, the regime gives tags a duration and therefore membership, and together they make the condition-split statistics reachable within weeks instead of never. The user's own hypothesis about cause is drawn beside the effect — the loop the AI currently owns alone.
Downsides
Detector false positives (compression lows, sensor noise) would spam ? marks without Δ, duration and no-anchor thresholds; the note sheet needs a prefill payload through the sheet coordinator; per-tag default durations are a semantics decision that leaks beyond the lab the moment the digest consumes bands.

7.

Cited facts — offline highlights; the AI may only narrate what it can cite

axis 5 · AI in the loop
Description
A pure highlights engine runs the detectors the app already has over the visible window — hypo episodes (returned as intervals, not counts), meal deltas ≥ 60, two boluses within DIA, exercise-adjacent drops, tight-control runs, band exits, regime overlaps — and emits up to five ranked, id-carrying facts anchored to timestamps, plus a feature sheet: WINDOW 14D N=3,912 · MEDIAN 08:00 142 · P95 19:00 251 · 3 HYPO EPISODES · SWEEPS 23 (14 CLEAN) · STRESSED 4 D. Every figure is a value type that cannot be constructed without its N. The lab draws numbered amber pins on the curve with a staged-reveal card list beneath, readable offline with no consent. The flagship card is the flight-recorder readout at a hypo onset: T-0 62 · IOB 1.8U · COB 0g · LAST BOLUS T-2h10 4U · EXERCISE T-1h05 45m WALK · HR 71→96 · TAG SLUGGISH. NARRATE (optional, consented) sends only the fact list — never readings — and the AI answers either with three plain sentences or with a closed annotation grammar (factId, kind, start, end, a ≤ 40-character label, tone) enforced by the JSON-schema output the app already uses and a strict decoder; any annotation that fails to cite an existing fact is dropped. The same feature sheet is the chart's accessibility descriptor, so VoiceOver gets a real chart summary.

A weaker variant — sending twelve glucose samples for an interval under the digest consent — was rejected for its privacy posture.
